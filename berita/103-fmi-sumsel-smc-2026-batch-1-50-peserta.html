SMC 2026 Batch 1 Tuntas: 50 Peserta Lulus Level 1, FMI Sumsel Sebut Nol Kecelakaan

SMC 2026 Batch 1 Closes: 50 Participants Pass Level 1, South Sumatra Reports Zero Accidents

Peserta Sriwijaya Mountaineering Course 2026 Batch 1 di Kawasan Belantara Keramasan, Palembang, Sabtu, 14 November 2026. (Foto: Dokumentasi FMI Pengprov Sumatera Selatan) Participants of Sriwijaya Mountaineering Course 2026 Batch 1 at the Belantara Keramasan area in Palembang on Saturday, November 14, 2026. (Photo: FMI South Sumatra documentation)

INDONESIA - Federasi Mountaineering Indonesia (FMI) Pengprov Sumatera Selatan menggelar Sriwijaya Mountaineering Course (SMC) 2026 Batch 1 pada Sabtu, 14 November 2026, pukul 08.00 sampai 17.00 WIB. Pelatihan Level 1 Basic Mountaineering itu diikuti 50 peserta dan berlangsung di Kawasan Wahana Outbound Belantara Keramasan, Palembang.

Pesertanya campuran. Ada anggota Mapala dan Sispala, kelompok pecinta alam, pemandu wisata, penyedia open trip, dan masyarakat umum. Komposisi itu berbeda dari diklat yang hanya diikuti kalangan mahasiswa pencinta alam, dan justru cocok dengan kondisi Sumatera Selatan, yang punya Gunung Dempo di Pagar Alam sebagai tujuan pendakian utama.

Tema yang diusung, First Step To The Mountain, dipasang bersama kalimat kerja yang lebih teknis: tingkatkan kapasitas, kelola risiko, menjelajah aman. Keduanya terlihat di papan tema yang dipasang di lokasi, dan di susunan acara yang membagi hari menjadi dua bagian: 40 persen teori, 60 persen praktik lapangan.

Sesi pagi berisi materi dasar: manajemen pendakian, logistik, dan pertolongan pertama di alam bebas atau wilderness first aid. Sesi siang peserta dibagi ke kelompok kecil beranggota tiga sampai lima orang. Di situ mereka mempraktikkan orientasi peta, penggunaan kompas, dan pemetaan manual, didampingi instruktur serta tim medis dan SAR yang siaga selama kegiatan.

Urutan pengajarannya mengikuti lima tahap: learn, practice, simulate, assess, certify. Artinya kelulusan ditentukan lewat penilaian di akhir, bukan hanya dari kehadiran di ruang kelas. Materi manajemen pendakian dan navigasi manual juga bukan bahan hafalan, karena di jalur Dempo peta sering tidak lagi sejalan dengan tanda alam yang terlihat.

Penyelenggara menyatakan seluruh rangkaian berjalan tanpa kecelakaan dan tanpa sampah yang tertinggal. Dua klaim itu disampaikan sebagai bagian dari laporan pelaksanaan, bukan hasil audit pihak ketiga, dan sebaiknya dibaca begitu.

Ketua Umum FMI Pengprov Sumatera Selatan, Muhammad Taufik, S.Kom., M.Kom., bersama Ketua Pelaksana M. Ilham, S.Tr.T., menyampaikan apresiasi kepada peserta dan panitia atas disiplin selama kegiatan. Keduanya menegaskan bahwa gunung dan alam bebas adalah ruang belajar yang menuntut kerendahan hati serta kesiapan teknis, bukan arena penaklukan.

Kegiatan ditutup dengan evaluasi praktik, sesi berbagi pengalaman, dan penyerahan sertifikat kelulusan. Sertifikat Level 1 itu menjadi modal kualifikasi untuk melanjutkan ke jenjang berikutnya di FMI, yaitu Intermediate atau Level 2.

Bagi FMI Sumsel, ukuran keberhasilan tidak berhenti di jumlah peserta yang lulus. Yang bisa diperiksa sesudah ini ada dua: berapa lulusan SMC Batch 1 yang benar-benar melanjutkan ke Level 2, dan apakah keterampilan navigasi serta pertolongan pertama itu dipakai saat mereka kembali ke jalur.

Pengprov itu bukan baru di kerja pelatihan. Pada 2025, FMI Sumsel mendampingi warga Dusun Talang Kubangan, Desa Bandar Jaya, Kecamatan Dempo Selatan, Pagar Alam, dengan materi pengelolaan ekowisata berbasis masyarakat, teknik dan keselamatan pendakian, serta kepemanduan wisata.

Artikel ini disusun dari keterangan tertulis FMI Pengprov Sumatera Selatan yang diterima Berita FMI, 17 November 2026, ditambah rincian materi dan lokasi dari pemberitaan sebelumnya (artikel 099). Foto: dokumentasi FMI Pengprov Sumatera Selatan.

INDONESIA - The Indonesian Mountaineering Federation (FMI) of South Sumatra held Sriwijaya Mountaineering Course (SMC) 2026 Batch 1 on Saturday, November 14, 2026, from 08.00 to 17.00 local time. The Level 1 Basic Mountaineering training drew 50 participants and took place at the Belantara Keramasan outbound area in Palembang.

The group was mixed. There were members of university and school mountaineering clubs, local outdoor groups, tour guides, open trip operators, and members of the public. That composition differs from courses limited to student climbers, and it fits South Sumatra, which has Mount Dempo in Pagar Alam as its main climbing destination.

The theme, First Step To The Mountain, was paired with a more technical working line: raise capacity, manage risk, explore safely. Both appear on the theme board at the venue and in a programme that split the day into two parts: 40 per cent theory, 60 per cent field practice.

The morning covered fundamentals: expedition management, logistics, and wilderness first aid. In the afternoon participants were split into small groups of three to five. There they practised map orientation, compass use, and manual mapping, supervised by instructors with a medical and search-and-rescue team on standby.

The teaching sequence follows five stages: learn, practice, simulate, assess, certify. Completion is decided by assessment at the end, not by attendance alone. Expedition management and manual navigation are not memorisation material either, because on the Dempo trail the map often stops matching the terrain in front of you.

The organisers stated that the entire programme ran without accidents and without waste left behind. Both claims were presented as part of the implementation report rather than a third-party audit, and should be read that way.

Muhammad Taufik, S.Kom., M.Kom., chair of FMI South Sumatra, together with programme director M. Ilham, S.Tr.T., thanked participants and committee members for their discipline throughout the day. Both stressed that mountains and wild places are learning spaces that demand humility and technical readiness, not arenas for conquest.

The programme closed with a practical evaluation, a sharing session, and the presentation of certificates. The Level 1 certificate serves as the qualification to continue to FMI's next stage, Intermediate or Level 2.

For FMI South Sumatra, success is not measured by how many participants passed. Two things can be checked afterwards: how many SMC Batch 1 graduates actually continue to Level 2, and whether they use their navigation and first aid skills when they return to the trail.

The provincial board is not new to training work. In 2025, FMI South Sumatra worked with residents of Talang Kubangan hamlet, Bandar Jaya village, Dempo Selatan subdistrict, Pagar Alam, on community-based ecotourism, climbing technique and safety, and tour guiding.

This article draws on written statements from FMI South Sumatra received by Berita FMI on November 17, 2026, plus material and venue details from earlier reporting (article 099). Photo: FMI South Sumatra documentation.
