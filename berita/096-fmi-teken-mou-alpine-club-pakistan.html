FMI Teken MoU dengan Alpine Club of Pakistan

FMI Signs MoU with Alpine Club of Pakistan

Suasana penandatanganan MoU antara Federasi Mountaineering Indonesia (FMI) dan Alpine Club of Pakistan di Kathmandu, Nepal, Sabtu (31/10/2026). (Dokumentasi FMI) The signing of the MoU between the Indonesian Mountaineering Federation (FMI) and the Alpine Club of Pakistan in Kathmandu, Nepal, Saturday (31/10/2026). (FMI documentation)

KATHMANDU, NEPAL - Federasi Mountaineering Indonesia (FMI) menandatangani Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) dengan Alpine Club of Pakistan (ACP) di Kathmandu, Nepal, Sabtu (31/10/2026). Penandatanganan berlangsung di sela-sela rangkaian Sidang Umum UIAA 2026. Kesepakatan itu masih berupa kerangka kerja: isinya menetapkan bidang yang akan dikerjakan bersama, bukan program yang sudah berjalan.

ACP adalah federasi nasional pendakian dan panjat Pakistan, organisasi nonpemerintah yang berdiri pada 1974 dan berkantor di Jinnah Stadium, Pakistan Sports Complex, Islamabad. Pakistan memiliki lima dari empat belas puncak dunia di atas 8.000 meter. Indonesia berdiri di sisi lain dengan gunung api di kawasan Ring of Fire, hutan tropis, dan komunitas pendaki yang tumbuh cepat. Dua medan yang berbeda itulah yang dipertemukan dalam naskah.

Butir yang paling konkret justru berada di hulu, sebelum pendakian dimulai. Naskah menyebut FMI akan menjalankan pemeriksaan kebugaran fisik, kesiapan teknis, pengalaman ketinggian, dan kepemilikan asuransi bagi warga Indonesia yang akan mendaki atau treking di Pakistan. Kedua organisasi juga sepakat menyiapkan sistem pemantauan bersama untuk mencatat warga Indonesia yang sedang berada di Pakistan. Prosedur maupun instrumennya belum ada, dan itu bagian yang harus disusun setelah penandatanganan.

Konsekuensinya berjalan ke hulu. Naskah menyebut ACP tidak akan mendukung permohonan izin pendakian, izin puncak, atau izin treking warga Indonesia yang tidak membawa surat rekomendasi resmi dari FMI. Bila butir ini diterapkan, jalur pendakian resmi ke Pakistan punya pintu di Indonesia, dan pemeriksaan awal berpindah dari agen perjalanan ke federasi nasional.

Bagian pelatihan dirancang dua arah. Rencana program bersama di Pakistan mencakup kedokteran ketinggian, panjat es, keselamatan longsoran, dan penyeberangan gletser. Sebaliknya, FMI akan menjadi tuan rumah bagi pembelajaran pendakian ketinggian tropis dan bertahan hidup di hutan. Standar keahlian dan sertifikasi direncanakan diselaraskan dengan kerangka internasional seperti UIAA, termasuk penyusunan kurikulum dan penilaian.

Ekspedisi bersama direncanakan menyasar jajaran Karakoram, Himalaya, dan Hindu Kush di Pakistan, dengan contoh puncak yang disebut dalam naskah: K2, Broad Peak, dan Nanga Parbat. Dari sisi Indonesia, sasarannya Puncak Jaya atau Carstensz Pyramid. Kerja sama pencarian dan penyelamatan masuk dengan bentuk yang sama: berbagi protokol dan modul pelatihan, serta koordinasi operasi bila pendaki Indonesia memerlukan bantuan di Pakistan atau pendaki Pakistan di Indonesia.

Isu konservasi juga masuk sebagai bidang kerja: pengelolaan sampah, prinsip Leave No Trace, dan mitigasi perubahan iklim di ekosistem gunung yang rapuh, termasuk lingkungan gletser. MoU berlaku tiga tahun sejak ditandatangani dan dapat diperpanjang. Setiap kegiatan rinci akan diatur lewat perjanjian tersendiri beserta penanggung jawab dari masing-masing pihak.

Dari pihak Indonesia hadir Ketua Delegasi FMI Brigjen TNI (Mar) Sandy Muchjidin Latief, S.I.P., M.M.; Sekretaris Jenderal dr. M. Iqbal El Mubarak, Sp.B-ET, FINACS; Wakil Sekretaris Jenderal Kolonel Marinir Arief Rahman Hakim, M.Tr (Opsla)., M.Han; Ketua Bidang Penelitian, Pengembangan, dan Inovasi dr. Putro Setyobudyo Muhammad, MBL, CIHL; dan Ketua Biro Hubungan Luar Negeri Zulfahmi Ramli. Dari ACP hadir Sekretaris Jenderal Ayaz Ahmed Shigri.

Yang menentukan sekarang adalah turunannya: prosedur penyaringan sebelum keberangkatan, alur penerbitan surat rekomendasi, dan jadwal program pertama. Sampai ketiganya disusun, belum ada satu pun butir yang bisa dipakai pendaki di lapangan. Bagi FMI, kesepakatan ini membuka pengalaman lapangan yang tidak bisa dipelajari dari gunung tropis saja: salju, gletser, dan medan es di atas 8.000 meter.

KATHMANDU, NEPAL - The Federasi Mountaineering Indonesia (FMI) signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Alpine Club of Pakistan (ACP) in Kathmandu, Nepal, on Saturday (31/10/2026). The signing took place on the sidelines of the 2026 UIAA General Assembly. The agreement is still a framework: it sets out the fields the two sides will work on together, not programmes already running.

ACP is Pakistan's national mountaineering and climbing federation, a non-governmental organisation established in 1974 and headquartered at Jinnah Stadium, Pakistan Sports Complex, Islamabad. Pakistan holds five of the world's fourteen peaks above 8,000 metres. Indonesia stands on the other side, with volcanoes along the Ring of Fire, tropical forests, and a fast-growing climbing community. Those two different terrains are what the document brings together.

The most concrete points sit upstream, before any climb begins. The document states that FMI will screen the physical fitness, technical readiness, high-altitude experience, and insurance coverage of Indonesian citizens planning to climb or trek in Pakistan. The two organisations also agreed to prepare a shared monitoring system to record Indonesian citizens currently in Pakistan. The procedure and its instruments do not exist yet, and that is the part to be built after the signing.

The consequence runs upstream as well. The document states that ACP will not endorse applications for climbing permits, peak permits, or trekking authorisations submitted by Indonesian citizens who do not carry a formal letter of recommendation from FMI. If that clause is applied, the official route to Pakistan has a gate in Indonesia, and the first check moves from travel agencies to a national federation.

The training section is designed to run in both directions. Joint programmes planned in Pakistan cover high-altitude medicine, ice climbing, avalanche safety, and glacier traversal. On the other side, FMI is to host the learning on tropical high-altitude climbing and jungle survival. Skill standards and certification are to be aligned with international frameworks such as the UIAA, including curriculum design and assessment.

Joint expeditions are planned for the Karakoram, Himalayan, and Hindu Kush ranges in Pakistan, with peaks named in the document including K2, Broad Peak, and Nanga Parbat. On the Indonesian side, the target is Puncak Jaya or the Carstensz Pyramid. Mountain search and rescue enters in the same form: sharing operational protocols and training modules, and coordinating operations when Indonesian climbers need assistance in Pakistan or Pakistani climbers in Indonesia.

Conservation is also listed as a field of work: waste management, Leave No Trace principles, and climate change mitigation in fragile mountain ecosystems, including glacier environments. The MoU runs for three years from the date of signing and may be renewed. Each specific activity will be set out in a separate project agreement with a designated coordinator from each party.

From Indonesia, the delegation comprised FMI Delegation Head Brig. Gen. TNI (Mar) Sandy Muchjidin Latief, S.I.P., M.M.; Secretary General dr. M. Iqbal El Mubarak, Sp.B-ET, FINACS; Deputy Secretary General Col. Marinir Arief Rahman Hakim, M.Tr (Opsla)., M.Han; Head of Research, Development and Innovation dr. Putro Setyobudyo Muhammad, MBL, CIHL; and Head of Foreign Relations Bureau Zulfahmi Ramli. From ACP, Secretary General Ayaz Ahmed Shigri attended.

What matters now is what follows: the pre-departure screening procedure, the flow for issuing recommendation letters, and the schedule of the first programme. Until those three exist, not a single clause can be used by climbers in the field. For FMI, the agreement opens field experience that tropical mountains alone cannot teach: snow, glaciers, and ice above 8,000 metres.
